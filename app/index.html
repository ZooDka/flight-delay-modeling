Timing is everything.

The same route is a different product at 7am than at 7pm. Delays pile up through the day as aircraft and crews fall behind, so an early departure is the single most reliable choice you can make.

Mean departure delay by scheduled hour, real flights. The afternoon window runs roughly five to seven times the delay of the early morning bank.

How it works

Departure delay is not a bell curve. It is a mixture: a large group of flights that leave on time or a little early, plus a long tail of flights that run seriously late. This tool models both parts together.

The delayed tail follows a log-normal shape. That was not assumed. Four candidate distributions were fit to real data and compared by AIC and a Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, and log-normal won on both. The fit was then checked on a held-out half of the data, with bootstrap confidence intervals on the percentiles.

From the fitted model, a seeded Monte-Carlo simulation runs in your browser to produce the distribution, the percentile spread, and the risk curve: the probability that the delay exceeds any number of minutes. A calibration check under the chart compares the simulated statistics back to your inputs, so you can see the model actually reproduces them.

The typical-flight profiles come from 336,776 real flights. When you enter your own flight's numbers, the same model is calibrated to match them, so it works for any flight in the world.
